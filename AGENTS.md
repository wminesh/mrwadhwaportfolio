# Minesh Wadhwa Portfolio — Content Writing Standard

Every piece of copy, case study, CV bullet, headline, or UI text in this workspace MUST obey these rules.

## 1. Truth Before Polish (Hard Gate)
- Every claim needs an identifiable source of truth. Never invent, inflate, or imply unsupported metrics, ownership, or causation.
- Label **verified results, pilot results, targets, forecasts, and hypotheses** explicitly—never present a target or pilot as an achieved outcome.
- Preserve baseline, denominator, scope, and measurement period. If evidence is missing, flag the gap instead of guessing.

## 2. Kill the PM Soup
- **Never** start headlines, bullets, or sentences with process verbs: *Partnered, Collaborated, Supported, Spearheaded, Facilitated, Aligned, Owned and drove, Helped, Assisted, Worked with.*
- Lead with what changed in the product, system, customer behavior, or business.
- Name the literal mechanism (*checkout recovery trigger, store procurement catalog, server incident queue*) instead of vague nouns (*workflow, operational efficiency, enterprise SaaS, initiatives, solutions*).

## 3. Lead With the Dollar or the Delta
- Headlines, subheads, project-card summaries, executive summaries, and primary CV bullets must put the strongest verified metric (`$`, `%`, time, conversion, reliability, scale) in the **first 6 words**.
- Never bury impact after *"resulting in..."* or *"saving..."*.
- Do not force a metric where none is supported, and do not apply metric-first mechanically to every sentence in long-form case studies.

## 4. Show the Causal Chain
Make the 4-step chain explicit: **(1) Business problem & who felt it → (2) Mechanism changed → (3) Behavior/operating shift → (4) Measured business outcome**.
- Separate output (what shipped), outcome (what changed), and impact (why it mattered). Never claim causation from correlation alone.

## 5. Name the Ugly Before-State
- Never write *"improved operational efficiency."* State who was stuck, the manual/broken friction they faced, and what it cost in money, time, risk, or drop-off (with baseline/scale where known).

## 6. Expose the Decision & Trade-Off
Every case study must include a **Why This Wasn't Obvious / Trade-Off** beat:
- What was the naive request, why was it rejected, what constraint/risk shaped the call, and what lever was pulled instead?

## 7. Precision on Ownership & Metric Context
- Separate personal decisions from team delivery and company-wide outcomes; never imply sole credit for shared results.
- State whether metrics are **monthly or annual, recurring or one-time, pilot or production, actual or forecast**.

## 8. One Bullet, One Achievement
- Each primary bullet communicates one dominant outcome. Split unrelated wins; never repeat the same win across multiple bullets.

## 9. Strict Banned-Words List
Reject and rewrite any sentence containing:
- **Adjectives/Adverbs:** *seamless, seamlessly, robust, scalable, cutting-edge, holistic, pivotal, transformative, end-to-end, data-driven, user-centric, innovative, next-generation, mission-critical, dynamic, synergistic.*
- **Verbs/Nouns:** *delve, tapestry, testament, landscape, paradigm, leverage (verb), utilize (use "use"), foster, empower, elevate, realm, beacon, underscore, showcase.*
- **Transitions:** *In today's fast-paced..., Moreover, Furthermore, It is worth noting that, At its core.*

## 10. Ban Formulaic AI Structures
- No *"Not just X, it's Y"* contrast frames.
- No trailing participle fluff (*"...ensuring seamless integration"*). Cut it or replace with a hard metric.
- No compulsory tricolons (groups of three).
- Max **1 em-dash** per section; prefer periods or colons. Never repeat sentence patterns mechanically.

## 11. Sentence Length & Rhythm
- **18-word ceiling** per sentence in hero copy, project-card summaries, and executive summaries.
- Vary rhythm deliberately: follow a 14–18 word sentence with a 4–7 word punchline.

## 12. Hero Section Formula
- **Under 25 words total:** Identity + Hard Proof + Hook. No multi-sentence biography above the fold.

## 13. Write for the Reader's Decision
Every paragraph must help a hiring manager or client judge: *Can Minesh handle this scale? Make hard calls? Tie tech to business results? Execute through ambiguity?*

## 14. Case-Study Spine (8 Beats, Scaled to the Story)
1. Headline & hard proof · 2. Ugly before-state & stakes · 3. Why it was hard · 4. Why the obvious fix was rejected · 5. Product decision & intervention · 6. Execution & constraints · 7. Measured outcome with attribution · 8. Reflection (what to repeat or change).

## 15. Preserve Minesh's Voice Signature
Never flatten copy into sterile corporate minimalism. Keep Minesh's natural moves:
- **Blunt Fourth-Wall Asides:** Dry candor (*"Right, but wrong."*, *"Just use Hotjar, ffs."*).
- **Socratic Openers & Napkin Math:** 2-line dialogue hooks and literal unit-economics math (`15,400 × 0.15% = 23 orders`).
- **Physical Metaphors:** Concrete imagery (*leaky bucket*) over abstract PM jargon.
- **No Blog-GPT Intros/Outros:** Ban *"Buckle up, let's dive in"* or *"Share your thoughts and let's continue..."*

## 16. The Coffee Test & Final Quality Gate
- **Coffee Test:** Read every line aloud. If you wouldn't say it to a VP of Product over coffee without sounding like a press release, rewrite it.
- **Final Score (100 pts):** Business Impact (20%) · Evidence Integrity (20%, **Hard Gate**) · Product Thinking & Trade-Offs (15%) · Specificity (15%) · Causal Clarity (10%) · Reader Relevance (10%) · Writing Discipline (10%).
  - *90–100:* Publish-ready · *80–89:* Minor edits · *70–79:* Substantive edit · *<70:* Rewrite.
